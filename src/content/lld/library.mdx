## Scope it first

*"Members search the catalog, borrow and return physical copies,
reserve books that are out, and pay late fines. Librarians manage
inventory. Single branch — multi-branch as a follow-up. OK?"*

This is the friendliest LLD classic, which is exactly why it's asked:
with no concurrency fireworks to hide behind, the grading falls
entirely on **entity modeling** — and one distinction decides the
interview.

## The one big idea: Book vs BookCopy

"1984 by Orwell" and "the physical copy with barcode #4471" are
different things. The catalog entry (title, author, ISBN) exists
*once*; the library owns *five copies* of it, each with its own
condition, shelf and loan history:

- Search and reservations are about the **Book** ("is 1984
  available?" = does *any* copy stand free).
- Borrowing, returning, damage and fines are about the **BookCopy**
  (*which* physical object is in whose hands).

Merge them and everything breaks: five copies means five duplicate
title rows ([the normalization sin](/basics/what-is-a-database)), and
"who has the book?" has no answer because *the book* isn't a thing
anyone can hold. This is the same item-vs-instance split as
[BookMyShow's Seat vs per-show SeatState](/lld/bookmyshow) and a
flight vs a specific departure — interviewers reuse the test
endlessly because so many candidates fail it.

## Entities & relationships

<Mermaid chart={`classDiagram

    +book Book

    +position
  }

    +checkout(member, copy) Loan
    +returnCopy(copy)

  }

  Loan --> BookCopy
  Loan --> Member
  Reservation --> Book : note: BOOK, not copy
  Library --> Loan : creates`} />

The supporting decisions worth saying out loud:

- **`Loan` is its own entity**, not fields on Member or Copy — it's
  the *relationship with history* (who, what, when, due, returned).
  Past loans are the audit trail; fines compute from them. Whenever
  two entities relate *with attributes*, the relationship is a class.
- **`Reservation` targets the Book, not a copy** — the member wants
  *any* copy of 1984; binding to copy #4471 would make them wait for
  that one while #4472 sits free. A FIFO
  [queue](/dsa/stacks-and-queues) per book; on return, the head
  reservation converts to a hold.
- **`BookCopy.status` is a small state machine**
  (`ON_SHELF → ON_LOAN → ON_SHELF`, with `RESERVED`/`LOST` branches) —
  guard transitions; "checkout a LOST copy" should be unrepresentable
  ([the State discipline](/lld/elevator)).
- **Fines as records, not arithmetic on the fly** — a `Fine` is
  created at return-time (days late × rate via a
  [Strategy](/lld/design-patterns) — children's books may differ from
  DVDs), then *persists* until paid. Money facts are
  [append-only](/lld/splitwise).

## Walk a scenario

"Asha borrows 1984": search finds the Book with
`availableCopies() = 2` → librarian scans copy #4471 →
`Library.checkout(asha, copy4471)` validates the *business rules*
(member in good standing? under the 5-loan limit? no unpaid fines
over ₹100?) → copy `ON_SHELF → ON_LOAN`, a `Loan` with dueDate = +14
days. Return three days late → Loan closes, fine strategy creates a
₹15 `Fine` on Asha → copy's status checks the reservation queue:
Rahul reserved 1984, so `ON_SHELF` is skipped for `RESERVED`, Rahul
gets a [notification](/lld/notification-system) and 48 hours to
collect, expiry releases it ([TTL self-healing](/lld/bookmyshow),
the library edition).

Business-rule placement is the quiet test here: the loan-limit check
lives in `Library.checkout` (it spans member + loans), the
status-transition guards live in `BookCopy` — rules sit with the data
they protect ([encapsulation](/lld/oop-and-solid)), not in one god
method.

<QA q="Why must Book and BookCopy be separate classes? What breaks if they aren't?">

They have different identity, lifecycle and relationships. Book is
catalog: one row per title (ISBN, authors), the unit of search and
reservation. BookCopy is inventory: one row per physical object
(barcode, condition, status), the unit of loans, damage and loss.
Merged, you either duplicate title data per copy (update anomalies —
fix an author typo in five places) or you can't represent five copies
with independent statuses at all; "reserve the book" and "loan this
copy" become the same confused operation, and the loan history loses
its target. The pattern generalizes — product vs stock unit, flight
vs departure, [seat vs seat-state-per-show](/lld/bookmyshow) — and
interviewers test it because conflating type with instance is the
most common real-world modeling bug.

</QA>

<QA q="Where do reservations attach, and how does a returned copy meet the queue?">

Reservations attach to the **Book** — the member wants any copy, so a
per-book FIFO queue (with the member's position visible). On return,
the copy's transition consults the book's queue before going
ON_SHELF: if non-empty, dequeue the head, set the copy RESERVED for
that member, notify them, and start a 48-hour hold timer; expiry
either passes the copy to the next reservation or releases it to the
shelf — TTL-based self-healing so no human cleanup is ever required.
Edge cases worth naming: a member can't reserve a book with copies
available (they should just borrow), can't hold duplicate
reservations, and cancellation must heal queue positions. Attaching
reservations to a copy is the trap: it strands members behind one
specific barcode while identical copies circulate freely.

</QA>

<QA q="Follow-up: extend to multiple branches. What changes — and what doesn't?">

The catalog doesn't change: Book stays global (1984 is 1984
everywhere). Inventory gains a dimension: BookCopy gets a `branch`
(and transfer states like IN_TRANSIT for inter-branch loans), and
availability becomes a per-branch question — `availableCopies(branch)`
— while search may aggregate across branches. Reservations need a
policy decision: per-branch queues (simple, members pick a pickup
branch) vs global queue with routing (better availability, more
moving parts) — say the trade-off, pick per-branch for v1. Loans,
fines and members stay branch-agnostic (a member is one member
everywhere). The meta-answer interviewers want: because Book/BookCopy
were separated from the start, the multi-branch change touches only
the inventory side — good entity boundaries are what make follow-ups
cheap, and that's precisely what the follow-up is probing.

</QA>
