## Drill these

Reveal each only after you've decided your structure and which story you'd use.
The answers here are *guidance*, not scripts.

<QA q="Tell me about a conflict with a coworker.">

Pick a real, low-drama disagreement and stay blameless. Structure: the
disagreement (a design or priority difference, stated fairly from both sides),
what *you* did to resolve it (listened to understand their concern, brought data
or a small prototype, found the shared goal), and the outcome (a decision both
could own, relationship intact). Avoid villain stories — they signal you'll be
hard to work with. The point is that you de-escalate and optimize for the team's
outcome, not for being right.

</QA>

<QA q="Tell me about a time you failed.">

Use a genuine failure with a real lesson (story #5 — silent fallbacks hiding
breakage). Own it without excuses, focus on what you learned and changed, and
show the change stuck (you applied it afterward). Don't pick a humble-brag
("I work too hard") or something catastrophic that questions your judgment —
pick a real mistake whose lesson made you measurably better.

</QA>

<QA q="What's your biggest weakness?">

Name a real one with an active correction, not a disguised strength. Example:
"I used to over-engineer for hypothetical scale; now I deliberately ship the
simplest thing that meets the actual constraints and revisit when the data
demands it." The structure is: the weakness → why it's a problem → the concrete
habit you adopted → evidence it's improving. Self-awareness + a system to improve
is what they're scoring.

</QA>

<QA q="Why are you leaving your current job / why this company?">

Stay positive about the present and specific about the future. Frame it as moving
*toward* something — more ownership, larger-scale systems, the depth an SDE2 role
offers — not running from a bad situation. For "why this company", cite something
concrete (the product, the technical challenge, the team's approach) so it's
clearly not a generic answer. Never bad-mouth a current employer; it reads as a
future liability.

</QA>

<QA q="Tell me about a time you had to meet a tight deadline.">

Show prioritization, not heroics. Structure: the constraint, how you scoped to
what mattered (cut or deferred the non-essential, communicated trade-offs early),
what you delivered, and that quality didn't silently suffer. Reach for the
StockVision prioritization story (#4) — choosing trust/depth over breadth under
pressure. The signal they want is judgment under constraint, not that you pulled
an all-nighter.

</QA>

<QA q="Tell me about a time you received critical feedback.">

Pick feedback you genuinely acted on. Structure: the feedback (stated plainly,
without defensiveness), your honest first reaction, what you changed, and the
result. It shows coachability — a top signal for SDE2, since you'll get a lot of
code review and design feedback. End with how you now seek feedback proactively
rather than waiting for it.

</QA>

<QA q="What are you most proud of?">

Choose something with real technical depth *and* impact you can speak to for ten
minutes of follow-ups — e.g. designing LandAI's provenance/compliance layer or
fixing StockStump's concurrency correctly. Explain why it was hard, the decisions
you made, and what it shows about you (judgment, ownership, care for correctness).
Pick something you can defend deeply, because they *will* drill in.

</QA>

<QA q="How do you handle disagreement with your manager on a technical decision?">

Disagree respectfully, commit once decided. Structure: you'd surface your concern
with reasoning/data privately, genuinely listen to constraints you might not see
(timeline, business context), and propose a small experiment or a reversible step
if possible. If the manager still decides otherwise, you commit and support it —
"disagree and commit". The signal: you advocate with evidence but you're not
obstructive, and you respect that some context sits above you.

</QA>
